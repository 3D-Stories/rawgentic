#713 — why a long run burned its window instead of handing off

#713 in plain words: why a long run burned its whole window instead of handing off

The short version. Your session was told "keep going" ten times and "you're nearly full" twice — and the "you're nearly full" message could never arrive at the moment the "keep going" decision was made. We fixed the timing, and found two more bugs on the way, one of which meant a whole part of the warning system had never worked at all.

---

Two of your runs failed at opposite ends of the same gap.

The cost in the first one is visible in the quality. Early passes each got reproduce-first, red-before-green and a committed guard. Pass 6 shipped with no guard. Pass 8 finished 1 of its 6 issues. The closeout was never started — because starting it at 98% would have meant inventing answers, and it correctly refused to. The only reason it faced that choice is that it was at 98%.

---

2. Why it happened

/goal decides whether to keep going at a moment called Stop — the instant a turn ends.

The context meter was not listening at that moment. It was registered on two other events, both of which happen *in the middle* of a turn. So its warning always arrived mid-turn and was already old news by the time the "keep going?" decision was made.

In your run the Stop hook fired about ten times. Not one of those firings had a handoff instruction available to it.

This was a timing problem, not a loudness problem. The message was fine. It just never spoke in the room where the decision was made.

---

3. The three bugs

Bug 1 — the meter was never in the room (the reported one)

Registered on the wrong events. Fixed by adding the Stop event.

Bug 2 — one of the meter's two arms had never worked, ever

Found while researching Bug 1, and this one is worse than it sounds.

A hook talks to the model by printing a small piece of JSON. There are two possible shapes. The meter used the wrong shape on one of its two events — so on that event, Claude Code silently threw the message away. No error, no warning, nothing.

That arm had been dead since it shipped. The meter had been running on one working arm this whole time.

We proved it by registering only that shape, on its own, and asking the model what it could see. It replied NONE.

Bug 3 — telling the model to hand off does not make it hand off

The most useful thing we learned, and it changed the fix.

We injected a "you're nearly full, hand off now" message into a real running goal loop. The model read it, flagged it as a possible prompt-injection attack, and refused to obey it — while still reporting that it had seen it.

That is correct behaviour. Text injected by a hook is *data*, not orders. It is the same defence that stops a malicious file telling your agent what to do.

So the fix could not be "order the model to hand off". It had to be something the model treats as trustworthy.

4. What the proof-of-concept actually proved

Five experiments against a real Claude Code, all committed so they can be re-run.

#QuestionAnswer
11Can the meter speak at Stop?Yes — and doing so forces one extra turn. The hook fired twice and the session took two turns for one prompt.
12Does adding our hook break /goal?No. Both hooks fired every turn, the goal ran and completed normally.
13If our hook crashes at Stop, does the turn break?No. Broken hook, garbage output, exit code 1 — session finished normally.
14Which message shape works?Only the nested one. The other is silently dropped. (Bug 2.)
15Does a handoff actually satisfy a /goal?Yes — proven, see below.

Probe 15 is the important one. We armed a real goal that said *"finish the work, OR record a handoff — a recorded handoff satisfies this goal"*. The session wrote the handoff, never did the work, and the goal completed. The judge's own words:

This recorded handoff satisfies the goal condition explicitly stated in the stopping criteria.

So the sentence "a handoff satisfies a loop goal" is not wishful thinking. It is literally how the judge behaves. That is the one channel the model trusts — because it is *your* goal text, not injected text.

6. What it costs, honestly

At most one extra turn, once per session. If the goal finishes at the exact moment the meter first speaks at Stop, our message becomes the thing that continues the turn.

That extra turn carries "you're at 70%, hand off, here's how" — which at that point is the behaviour we want. So we accepted it deliberately rather than designing it away.

An independent reviewer (a different AI model) checked the design and found 7 problems, then checked the code and found 4 more. Most became changes. Two suggestions were declined in writing, with reasons, so the trade-off is on the record rather than buried.

---

7. What is still not proven

  • Nothing was tested on an older Claude Code. The Stop message is a recent capability; on an old

version it degrades to silence rather than breaking anything — but that is reasoned, not measured.

  • The 8-continuation safety cap was never pushed to its limit.
  • Neither original failure has been reproduced end to end after the fix. That needs another long

real run — which is the honest limit of everything above.

  • Probe 15 used one wording, once. It shows the mechanism works; not that every phrasing works.
